Processing Pipeline

Last Updated: September 1st, 2026

PHASE 1: CLEANING AND VALIDATING RAW DATA

Step 1

Manual standardisation of variable columns directly relevant to the analysis; the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) and Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) code columns.

The exported CSV is also converted to Parquet for more efficient data storage and loading.

Step 2

Clean and validate variable columns relevant to geocoding and spatial annotation.


  1. Optionally validate addresses against the USPS Addresses database and compress them to remove typographical errors.
  2. Use validated addresses to verify geocoordinates (longitude/latitude) via the U.S. Census Bureau Geocoder API.
  3. Use the resulting geocoordinates for spatial joining against U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line Shapefiles for census boundary annotation, including GEOIDs (block group, tract, county, state) and Core Statistical Areas (CBSA and CSA).

Step 3

Reshaping and annotation of the cleaned and validated data in preparation for metric calculation over unique American Business Identifier (ABI)/address combinations.


  1. Normalise SIC codes across unique ABI/address entries and translate them into religious affiliations (e.g., Hindu, Judaism, Christian).
  2. Fill in missing years-open values between filings shorter than the closure threshold.
  3. For ABIs filed under more than one address, annotate relocation metrics: number of moves; mean and maximum haversine distance (km) between addresses; and a Boolean flag indicating whether the move exceeded 5, 10, or 25 miles.
PHASE 2: Metric Generation

Three metrics were requested for dashboard visualisation; all remaining metrics were intended to generate insights from the aggregation tables. All metrics are calculated across three stratifications: census boundary, religious affiliation, and decennial year (2000, 2010, and 2020).

Metrics are first calculated at the block group level, the smallest census boundary, then aggregated upward to coarser boundaries, with values summed, averaged, or maxed at each step; this process is referred to as a rollup. Only GEOIDs are rolled up; ZCTA were not, as they do not crosswalk to census boundaries.

Openings
Total ABIs located in the unit/window, including those later excluded by a missingness flag.
Closures & Reopenings
Closure events (4+ inactive years following activity) and reopenings (2+ active years following a closure) are reported as counts per any event, per sq. mi., per 10k residents, average number of events, and max number of events.

Each is reported for no_moves (excludes multi-address ABIs) and all (addresses for an ABI column-wise summed).
Relocations
Count of ABIs that moved, average moves per ABI, weighted-average and max move distance (km), and counts exceeding 5/10/25-mile thresholds.
Missingness flags
Address missing, address is a PO Box, or GEOID/ZCTA missing.
USAGE NOTES

Refer to Code/ for all scripts related to the pipeline. All files designed for the "2026 Formatted" dataset represent the most current version; any "2023 Formatted" scripts are retained for posterity, as they were used for the 2025 Summer prototype.


Not all data used in this process can be or was shared. Users must consult their institution on how to gain access to the Data Axle business data processed here. All other ancillary data sources, such as the U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line Shapefiles, are listed with access instructions in the Data/Raw/ and Data/Results/ README.md files.


The pipeline assumes the data is cleaned and validated anew and does not include a pathway for incorporating new yearly releases. While this functionality was not a priority in the current iteration, it can be incorporated in Phase 1, Step 2, reducing the time and financial costs associated with API validation.
